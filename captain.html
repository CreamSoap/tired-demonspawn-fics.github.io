chapter 1: Of unnamed Captains and badass married men.

Grace Ashcroft was always fascinated with the world of bioterror.

She supposed it came with growing up surrounded by the most diabolical industry secrets just a few feet away from her at all times, ready to be exposed in a scathing article.

Yes, her mom hid them very well and never let her see anything that could make her into a target, but it wasn’t like she didn’t know what her mother wrote about. It wasn’t a secret between them.

So, from a young age, she found herself on forums, reddits and whatever else, joining discussions about the latest attacks.

Like the New York A-GUA tanker attack, that one was so cool, specifically because there was now a lot of New Yorkers that could tell you what it was like to be a B.O.W. first hand. (the local therapists had to make bank after)

Or the Bakers’ family series of disappearances… Though she didn’t like thinking of that particular forum discussion after… Anyway. Grace knew all these incidents had people behind them. Not only as perpetrators but as the heroes that saved the day.

So, when she eventually joined the FBI as an analyst, there was a part of her just giddy at the thought of having access to some of the case files associated with these incidents; knowing just a little more about how the day was saved.

She of course took her job seriously, not even attempting to look into anything that wasn’t strictly related to her investigations in the first few months, but once she did, she came to know them.

See, whenever there would be a crisis big enough for the BSAA to step in, a rather funny fact of their organization would make itself known…

The first letter of an operative’s surname was all Grace had access to, due to all the redacting in what little files the BSAA shared with the American government. And by reading through several of the ‘big’ cases(not so big to be completely redacted, but big enough to warrant people of lower clearance having access to the available information), Grace realised with no small amount of humour that most of their top operatives’ names started with the letter R.

New York, China, Africa, hell even what little the BSAA shared of the Bakers, all overseen and resolved by a mythical BSAA R operative or Captain.

It has crossed her mind before that maybe even the initial was fake, literally standing for ‘redacted’ but she didn’t exactly have many synonyms that would explain the other blacked out names.

Sure, Africa’s operative A could stand for ‘amended’ but Lanshiang’s operative N and Italy’s special agent V stumped even Grace’s skill with a thesaurus.

Which led her to believe the BSAA just really liked people with last names starting with R.

Though, whenever she was feeling down, she liked to imagine all the different Commanders and Captains and Operatives as the same mythical “superhero” BSAA soldier. It felt nice to imagine (if only for a moment) that wherever a bioweapon would spring up there would be an immovable soldier to meet its unstoppable force, saving the day.

But of course, no one person could do all that.

“You have a message from Captain Redfield.”

The name didn’t even register as special through the wave of relief going through Grace; they were being rescued, they were going to make it.

Leon sighed beside her, his shoulders relaxing, clearly recognising the name, “Lay it on me.”

The helmeted man chuckled, “You’re lucky you’re alive because I’m going to kill you.”

Grace stiffened at the threat briefly, before hearing Leon let out a laugh of his own, “Yea yea, tell him I love him too.”

Leon got up with a grunt and then helped Grace up as well, putting a reassuring hand on her back, “Easy, these guys are friendlies.”

He turned back to the helmeted man, motioning to Grace’s injured side vaguely, “Prep medical, got an SI. Dr. Ugly stabbed her with a tentacle.”

Only once outside and miles away, sitting in an ambulance, wrapped in a trauma blanket and cup in hand did the name Redfield spark curiosity in Grace. Leon just gave her the news that Emily might just make it out as well when the BSAA logo on… everything, really, registered.

“I thought you were DSO.”

Leon’s eyebrows briefly shot up before he processed her unspoken question, his hands idly working on ungloving themselves, throwing the pieces of leather on the ground uncaringly.

“Oh, these are Chris’ guys. He’s the uh. Captain Redfield.” he looked around conspiratorially, smirking, before he pulled out a simple silver band from one of his back pockets and put it on his finger, wiggling the now ring wearing left hand cheekily, “Redfield-Kennedy, between you and me… and the state of New York I guess.”

“Oh! Okay.”

Diversity win! Two apparently incredible men in the fight against bioterrorism were gay married. Huh. Actually yea that was quite the win.

And then Grace realised: Redfield. She probably just got rescued by one of the legendary R Captains. Privately she hoped it was the one that worked on the Bakers incident, there were a few things she really wanted to ask him about.

The sound of a helicopter drew near, Leon tracking it in the sky behind the ambulance, “And while we’re sharing secrets, I don’t usually wear this at work, but I need a peace offering via a reminder that he decided to put a ring on it.”

The helicopter touched down, the rotors still spinning when the doors slid open, revealing a massive man. Maybe this was boulder punching (former, now promoted) Operative R?

He stopped by the helmeted operative from before, exchanging a few words before being pointed to the ambulance, Leon waving softly as the man turned towards them fully, striding in their direction with a truly terrifying set to his brows. It made Grace freeze a little, though as Leon really didn’t seem in any sort of fear of what his husband might do to him, she tried to relax herself by joking, “Heh, w-well you could have picked a better murder witness than me.”

Leon huffed a laugh, slightly turning towards her, “Please, there’s no one I’d have as witness to my murder than the bravest FBI agent I know.”

Grace blushed a little at the praise, but before she could respond the unstoppable Redfield finally deemed he was close enough to yell.

“LEON!”

Leon put his hands up placatingly, “That’s my name.”

Chris closed the last few feet and grabbed Leon by his collar, seemingly planning to lift him up, before something broke in his expression and he stilled, his brows still set terrifyingly and hands clutching the collar’s fabric.

“What the hell were you thinking Kennedy, a fucking mission to Raccoon City of all places? In your condition?” his shoulders shook slightly, his anger slowly cracking to reveal worry, “You know how terrifying it was to find you gone after having to pick you up passed out in the shower the day before?”

He let go of Leon’s collar and started checking him over, “And fighting a nemesis? Leon, you can’t do this to me, lot of my family is fucking dying, you included, and you decide the-”

He stopped, stunned, holding Leon’s hands, staring at the soft pink web of scarring which replaced the sickly black.

“The illness. Is it? Does this mean?” Chris’ voice wobbled dangerously.

“Cured. More than bad memories in this place turns out.” Leon’s voice was carefully steady.

Grace couldn’t see his face, but she suddenly realised Leon probably also put the ring on for his husband’s sake: to solidify that it’s really him. That he’s alive.

“Oh thank god.” Chris enveloped the other man in a tight embrace, murmuring something Grace couldn’t make out into Leon’s neck, Leon replying in the same hushed tone as he hugged back.

Grace looked away from them awkwardly. She hasn’t known Leon or this Chris guy for long, but they both seemed very anti PDA. At least while at work. So the intimacy of this hug was therefore a bit much. Hell, it probably would have felt a little much even if they were the most pro PDA people ever.

She busied herself by once again thinking about something that has been bugging her all night: Leon Kennedy. Surely not of the Kennedy Report, right? Grace hasn’t read it, but she knew of it. She was pretty sure everyone who was in any way involved on interested in bioterror knew of it.

Funnily enough, reading anything even vaguely associated with the DSO, basically FBI’s sister organisation, was akin to just turning her monitor off, Grace’s level of clearance allowing her access to only the names of files (sometimes not even those) and the vaguest of declassified information. Whereas she could read most BSAA files just fine, save a redacted name or detail here or there, despite them not being associated with the government at all.

So while Grace somewhat understood that Leon was probably a big deal, she couldn’t quite get as exited about it as about the potential of which Captain R Chris could be.

The men broke apart, Chris finally acknowledging Grace, eyes still shining with tears Grace politely ignored as she snapped her attention back to them, “Is this the involved FBI agent?” his posture and tone were serious again, the sudden switch up almost gave Grace whiplash.

“Um. A-Analyst. Actually.”

Leon nodded as his husband’s eyes softened at the word analyst; probably not a fan of field agents, “Uhuh, she was kidnapped by the bad guys and,” he paused dramatically, “she’s the one who figured out how to cure us.”

Grace blushed at the praise, looking away, fidgeting with the lukewarm cup in her hands, “I-I didn’t really figure it out. Um. Spencer did. I j-just put in a password.”

Chris looked lost in thought for a moment, “Spencer…” he sounded a thousand miles away, before he shook his head, breaking himself out of whatever trance he was in, taking a step towards her, as Leon huffed a laugh next to him, shaking his head knowingly.

“Can I hug you?”

Oh. Grace didn’t expect that request. She expected something more interrogation flavoured. But well. It kinda made sense? He was just told she was the reason his husband was alive.

She awkwardly put the cup away and opened her arms, “Erm… Sure?”

And as she got picked up from the ambulance in a bear hug, blanket and all, held against a massive and warm chest and thanked tearily about saving the lives of people whose names she tried her best to remember, which BSAA Captain R Chris was suddenly wasn’t on her mind at all.

chapter 2: Welcome to the family… other daughter.

Start note:

Who knew trying to adopt someone classified as a B.O.W. would be such a pain.

It would all be so worth it for Emily to have a proper home and life, but Jesus Christ. All this paperwork might just kill Grace. Not zombies, not weird white-haired men with Matrix powers, hell, not even the 7ft tall man that mutated into an actual room of flesh after cutting her side really bad, but the wording of official documentation she needed to fill out to be able to properly adopt Emily.

“We’re technically blood related right? Couldn’t we use that?”

Leon, bless his heart, sat next to her on her couch, squinting at one of the papers through a pair of reading glasses, suffering the pain of administrative hoop jumping with her, “I mean I guess? Might just make things more complicated though, you’re not exactly related… normally. I’d have to call a few people to tell you how proving relation to an illegally made clone of you works.”

Grace sighed, he was right, best not to bring up the clone thing at all.

He lifted up another paper, the one that came in the mail the previous morning, “Oh great, Rebecca already fully cleared her as being cured and normal, that should speed things up.” He started rummaging through the pile of papers half filled out on the coffee table before them.

Rebecca? Oh right. The letter was from a Professor Rebecca Chambers. Though when Grace first looked at it she was more focused on the quite frankly ridiculous amount of Ph.D.s after the name to focus on the name itself.

Leon finally gathered up all the papers he was looking for, carelessly tossing them over his shoulder, “Okay that means we don’t need all these and we just have to focus on the fact she doesn’t legally exist.” He looked behind himself at the scattered papers, “At least I think so…”

Grace nodded, “Okay…” She paused before sighing, “Thank you for doing this with me, I really wouldn’t even know where to start with this.”

Leon waved a hand dismissively, “Don’t thank me yet, I barely know what I’m doing myself.”

“Really?” She was genuinely surprised, when she called him out of pure desperation at 2am after a full evening of parsing through legal documentation he sounded so confident in helping her ‘adopt a helpless blonde… that’s an inside joke, don’t mind me.’

“Pff- yea, really. I’m mostly working with years of parsing through weird government paperwork talk here; despite my best efforts and what my friends say I have yet to legally adopt anyone.” He made a strange expression, “Well. Not on my own anyway.”

He paused for a bit, before his eyebrows shot up in a clear epiphany, “Tell you what. I think we earned ourselves a little celebratory dinner. For everything. And Chris knows way more about the legal proceedings regarding B.O.W.s, so. I say you take a much deserved nap and stop by our place today at…” He looked at his watch, then glanced at her apartment window where it was clear the sun was well on its way to being up, his expression pinched with the typical post all-nighter ‘fuck I didn’t mean to be awake this long’ regret. “Seven. Let’s say seven. Seven sounds good.”

Oh. Wait. She was being invited over for dinner. She was going to have dinner with Leon and his husband who was totally one of the BSAA R Captains.

“Oh, um. Should I? Bring anything?” You brought things when invited to someone’s home, right?

“No alcohol.” Leon shot almost immediately, then paused in thought, “Other than that do whatever, we don’t have any allergies to worry about, don’t feel pressured tho, this is pretty last minute… Do you? …Have any allergies I mean?”

There was an implication to automatically and thoughtlessly banning alcohol from a gathering that itched at Grace’s mind, but she decided not to really think about it.

“I don’t.”

Leon clapped his hands together, smiling, “Well that’s that! Now I think we both have a bed that’s calling our name.” He started getting up, pocketing his reading glasses in a smooth motion.

A nap sounded heavenly right now.

“Yeah, I guess. See you in the evening?”

He put on his coat, it was a bit bigger on him, Grace idly thought it must be Chris’, “See you there, kiddo. I’ll text you the address when I get home.”

Shit, right, the address, she would have totally forgot to ask.

“Okay. Um. Bye.” She waved awkwardly to his finger salute as he left.

Grace didn’t belong here. That was the first thought she had after rounding the corner that led to a quaint little suburb.

But rechecking the address for the 8th time and finally parking her little moped in the driveway of a rather ordinary looking house, she had no choice but to accept she was at the right place.

She took out the plastic case of storebought tiramisu that she got to not feel as awkward(which it failed at) from her moped’s storage and made her way to the door, the ornamental sign reading ‘Redfield’ with a smaller laminated ‘& Kennedy’ taped to the bottom of it above the doorbell assuring her she was at the very least at the correct house, but not making her nerves lessen any.

She pressed the doorbell, hearing a bit of commotion behind the door and a sound of tiny but fast steps(?) getting closer. Did they have a dog or something? Leon seemed like the type to warn about that or at least ask about dog allergies while they were on the subject. Plus, it sounded like the steps were on tile, or at least some kind of hard floor, a dog or most other animals would have claws that would scrape against that.

The mail slot opened but closed again before Grace could see who or what opened it. Then came the fiddling with the deadbolt, like it took whoever was opening it a few tries to successfully turn it.

Only once the door was opened and Grace had to look down and down to meet the bright blue eyes of a tiny little girl did she consider the ‘Not on my own’ comment Leon made earlier.

“Um. H-Hi.” Said Grace.

The little girl stared at her. It would have been creepy were it not for the plastic tiara on her head and the colourful outfit that clashed horribly in a way that could only work on a small kid.

Wait did something move under her skin right there?

But then the little girl smiled a wide smile filled with a few missing teeth, “Hi! You’re blonde too! That’s so cool!”

“Y-Yea…” shit, what did you talk to children this small about, “I… like your tiara?”

It seemed that was the correct answer as the little girl giggled and struck a few poses, though they were all clashing a little with the tiara and rainbow skirt as they were more poses a bodybuilder would strike, “Isn’t it cool? Chris got it for me.” Not dad, papa, or something else, just Chris.

The man in question finally made himself known, rounding the corner with an expression of fear, before he saw it was just Grace at the door and looked to the little girl with a concerned look, “Rosemary! You can’t open doors to strangers.”

‘Rosemary’ looked back at him with a pout, “But Leon said an awkward girl would come over and then I looked through the letter hole and there was an awkward girl.”

Chris kneeled down, still towering over the little girl and grabbed her shoulders gently, jeez, he could basically wrap his hands around her entire torso, “I know sweetheart, but she’s still a stranger, okay?”

The little girl wilted, truly living up to her floral name, “Okay…”

“Hey, it was who we were expecting so just remember it for next time, hm? Now go and make sure Leon doesn’t set the kitchen on fire, alright?” he smiled warmly at her and okay there was no way she wasn’t his, right? Grace was pretty sure this kind of warmth only came from parents.

Little Rosemary perked up again, “’Kay!” and skipped off, Chris following her with a fond expression, before he turned back to Grace, getting up, his expression turning less warm.

It was still welcoming, but clearly wary of her. They were relative strangers after all.

“Sorry about her, come in.” He wrung his hands a little awkwardly (which made Grace feel a little better) before closing the door behind her, glancing at the box of tiramisu she held in her hands and perking up a little, “Oh, you didn’t need to bring anything. Let me take that.”

“Oh. Um. Here.” She handed it over, noticing his left hand was now also decorated with a simple silver band that matched Leon’s.

“Leave your coat over on the hanger, shoes in the rack.” He nodded with his head at the spots, “Dinner is almost done, you came perfectly on time, hope the trip was okay.”

Grace took off her winter coat and shoes quickly, stammering out an apology, “The d-drive was fine. S-Sorry for making you host m-me on short notice like this.”

Chris smiled good naturedly, “You didn’t make me do anything. I’m sorry Leon invited you on short notice like this.”

He motioned for her to follow deeper into what was presumably his family’s house, if the age of the doorbell sign was anything to go by.

“Y-Yes well, Leon said you had more experience with um… paperwork around B.O.W.s?”

He shot her a little more serious look over his massive shoulder, before nodding, his tone still warm despite the look, “After dinner.”

They walked into a kitchen/dining room, the two room parts separated by a counter, the room branching off to another hallway on one side and an archway that lead to a living room based on the couch that peeked out on the other.

Leon was currently locked in a staring contest with the bubbling pot of what would most likely be dinner, while Rosemary skipped around the table, setting it with cutlery.

Leon glanced back at them before doing a double take and turning fully, walking towards Grace, clapping her on the shoulder with a smile, “Hey kid, glad you could make it.”

“Thanks for having me.” She shifted from foot to foot, avoiding Leon’s gaze, Chris walking past them and putting the tiramisu in the fridge.

“Hey, you need a break, plus I gotta start the long process of repaying you somehow.” He guided her to the table, pulling out a chair for her.

“Oh y-you don’t have to. Repay me with a-anything.” He really didn’t, she honestly thought she repaid him for saving her throughout the night with elpis.

“Yea but I want to, how ‘bout that huh? Can’t have a rebuttal against that.” He replied with the confidence of someone extremely familiar with people not accepting gratitude, making Grace shut up and stare at the woven placemat in front of her.

Rosemary sat next to her, staring up at her with those big eyes and a bigger grin, “Hi. I’m Rose. I like your shirt. My favourite colour is green. I’m six and one qua- qu…” she lost her steam a little, face scrunching up.

“Quarter?” Grace supplied, “Yea!” Rose lit up again.

Grace felt a little awkward, “Oh um. Nice to meet you Rose. I’m Grace. I… like your shirt too.” it had a stegosaurus on it, it was actually pretty rad, “My favourite colour is red and I’m twenty-five.”

“Wow! You’re old! Not as old as Chris and Leon tho. They’re old.”

Leon chuckled, sitting across from Grace, “I’ll have you know little lady I’m still in my prime. Took down a giant monster just two weeks ago.”

Rose blew a raspberry at him, “Pff- sure you did.”

Grace felt a little bad, “No he did, I was there.”

Rose looked up at her in wonder, “Really?”

“Yep! He’s really cool y’know. Your um… Leon.” She still wasn’t sure how were they… related, exactly.

“Well if Grace with the cool shirt says it, it must be true.” Chimed in Chris, putting down several pots on the table.

“You’re bi- bia- hnng…” she got frustrated again, before reframing her statement entirely, “Leon is your favourite Chris, of course you’d take his side!”

For a moment Grace swore Rose’s eyes glowed. Huh, what kind of lighting are they using? Weird for it to catch in her eyes like that.

Chris acted mock hurt, “Accused of favouritism. By my own daughter.”

Okay so she was their daughter. Probably via adoption based on Leon’s comment. That’s probably why she calls them by their names. They don’t want to erase her biological parents maybe?

Rosemary blew another raspberry, at Chris this time, before being handed a deep plate of macaroni with some kind of very nice smelling brown sauce with meat bits, which she took without complaint.

“I guess if Grace says it then Leon probably did do it. Even if he’s old.”

The food came and went, finished with her mediocre dessert, accompanied by minimal awkward conversation along the lines of ‘so what do you do for work’ and ‘which dinosaurs do you think Chris could beat up’ and soon it was just Grace and Chris at the table, Leon leaving to prepare Rose for bed.

As awkward as the first few minutes alone were, once Chris got a hold of all the paperwork Grace brought with her, he started going through it like a well-oiled machine. Leon was right, he really did know more about all the legal mumbo jumbo.

After a bit he frowned at one of the papers slightly, “Do you have the B.O.W. safety paperwork?”

“O-Oh, yea, here.” She reached into her bag, pulling out the papers Leon threw away in the morning, “I thought Leon said we’d only need to focus on her… not legally existing. Because of the clearance from um… Rebecca?”

Chris took it, already marking out a few checkboxes on the new papers as he explained, “Well yes, that will be your biggest battle, however once classified as a B.O.W. you have to always fill out the B.O.W. related paperwork. Even if the B.O.W. in question is proven harmless.”

He sighed, “It’s a necessary evil, I suppose, though personally I don’t see why it couldn’t be solved with the mandatory medical checks. They’re going to happen anyway, why do you need a separate piece of paper that says the exact same thing as the doctor’s note.”

Grace nodded, thinking.

“Rose isn’t… normal, is she.” She found herself saying.

The scratching of Chris’ pen stilled, the atmosphere around them turning heavy and dangerous. Chris kept his eyes fixed on the paper he was preoccupied with before, but Grace could simply feel him raring up for a fight to protect his adopted daughter. Like he was mentally mapping out where he would hide Grace’s body so Rose’s secret would remain safe.

Grace reeled, realising what she just said, “Ohmygod I’m so sorry t-that sounded so bad. I-I d-d-didn’t mean. W-Well I did mean-”

Chris interrupted her with a chuckle, the oppressive atmosphere melting away with a snap, “No you’re right, she isn’t. She’s… special.”

His eyes turned a bit sad as he resumed writing out details into one of the papers with surprisingly neat letters, “She’s also still… in a less stable position with the government than we’d like. Sorry if I don’t share details. Unlike Emily she isn’t exactly… safe. Both from certain organisations and as a B.O.W.”

Rosemary being a dangerous B.O.W.? But she was so… Normal. Despite Grace’s previous words.

“N-No its fine, really. I understand. Don’t w-worry, her secret is safe with me.”

He smiled a genuine smile at her, “Thank you.”

He paused in thought briefly, “I can tell you how we got her. Well… in the broadest of strokes.”

That got Grace’s attention, she would lie if she said she wasn’t dying to know how did the little bundle of joy come into their lives.

Chris continued, “I suppose it was kind of like your experience. A place full of B.O.W.s and a little girl in need of rescue… I just… I wish I was the one who… maybe then Ethan would still be… sorry, wow, that story got away from me immediately, heh.” he laughed humourlessly.

Ethan? Like Ethan Winters the survivor of the Bakers that eventually stopped very systematically being mentioned in reports and by all accounts fell off the face of the Earth? No, Grace pay attention, how could the thought of ‘Captain R’ even cross her mind right now? She was clearly having a bonding moment with the husband of the guy she trauma bonded with a few weeks ago. Stupid brain. Stupid.

“It’s okay I-I don’t mind, you don’t need to tell me anything, a-anyway.”

“No, I want to finish… There’s not many people that know. And you… I trust you with this.”

Grace gulped nervously, she understood the weight of such a statement.

Chris took a steadying sigh, “Her father saved her. But in taking down the source of the B.O.W.s in the area he took way too much damage himself. He trusted me with Rose in his final moments.”

“I’m sorry… did you know him well?” It felt right to ask, he seemed too shaken up by it for the dad to be just a random civilian. Plus, if you were some badass dad saving your daughter from B.O.W.s you probably wouldn’t trust just anyone with the daughter in question.

“Yea. I did. We became friends after he found himself in the middle of another B.O.W. outbreak. In Luisiana, actually.”

Grace’s mind was going at mach speeds. Holy shit. It was Ethan Winters from the Bakers incident. Chris was Captain R of the Bakers incident. Calm down. She could ask him all the questions she had later. They were bonding right now.

But…

“…Was it the Bakers incident?” She had to make sure.

Chris seemed taken aback, “Oh.. um.. yea. Sorry, I don’t know how much the wider public knows, I’m… surprised you even know about it. Sorry if I don’t share any more details.”

“N-no that’s fine I was just m-making sure. S-see I’ve just always been surrounded by research into bioterrorism d-due to my mom and the Bakers was j-just such a blatant coverup w-with the leak of chemicals that h-had no business being in a L-Luisiana swamp and my mom wrote about it actually, s-so I had more reputable sources available to m-me and then when I j-joined the FBI I saw just h-how detached the BSAA was in t-the report despite clearly being involved and y-you mentioned Ethan and I know that one of the two survivors were Mia and Ethan W-Winters and t-they disappeared a-and o-oh my gosh I’m talking too much…” She clapped her hands over her ears and curled into a ball in the kitchen chair feeling her entire head burning with embarrassment.

Great going Ashcroft, amazing 2nd-ish impression on one of the totally awesome Captain Rs that also happened to be married to the guy that casually walked up to her hospital bed when they were both in quarantine and wrote himself down as her emergency contact into her chart like he was supposed to be there and him not being there was a logistical error made by the hospital.

A giant warm hand rested on her back, making her look up, meeting the eyes of Chris, who was smiling at her warmly, “Hey, it’s fine.”

He looked up from her, facing the hallway, his face falling slightly, followed by a defeated sigh as he hung his head, the hand on Grace’s back still a comforting weight.

“Okay Leon you win.”

Grace looked to the hallway as well, where Leon was now leaning against the wall, smirking, “I said she would grow on you.”

“W-What?” Huh? Win? What?

Leon walked over to them, taking a seat next to Grace, his hand joining Chris’ on her back, “You’re part of our little family now, my condolences.”

“O-Oh.” Grace didn’t quite understand what was being said to her right now.

“Yep, taking care of you no matter what, no take-backsies, you made it through the first half of the Redfield veto. And trust me, this one is way more paranoid than the second half. So, you’re basically approved already.”

Grace had been doing good. Great even.

Her job was going fantastic: apparently Jill and her squad put in a good word for her, so her status as more or less the FBI’s go-to for sharing info with the BSAA got utilised a lot more, with which came a higher clearance and a raise, which then meant she was finally able to afford that tiny car to better transport herself and Emily.

Speaking of Emily! She was finally hers; the court hearings and paperwork all came through and she was now officially Emily Ashcroft, new fully legal birth certificate to go with it and everything.

And she was doing amazing in her classes. Her remedial tutor said that it could be less than a year before she could be up to date on what a 9-10 year old(the physical age the doctors settled on, which became her legal birth year) (hey, just because the certificate was legit didn’t mean all the things in it were 100% true) should know and, if Grace and Emily wanted to, could probably start attending a normal school. And Emily for her part was always excitedly sharing just what new thing she learned.

Grace also had a supportive found family that had slowly trickled into her life, always there to help her and she, in turn, always there to help them.

By all accounts her life was going the best it had ever been.

So why was she feeling like this?

Every sound, every scrape, every stray gust of wind had her looking over her shoulder and itching for her service gun, ready to draw it at a shambling zombie.

It wasn’t like with her mom; she didn’t lose anyone this time. That meant it should be fine, right? All the different ‘family traumas’ that Grace knew still haunted her… aunts and uncles? Hell, this was the first time since she started suspecting Chris of being just the one lone Captain R when they were alone. Perfect opportunity to broach the kind of conversation topic that might stir up bad memories for him.

Carpet? Wasn’t she in the tiled dining room area?

She raced into a hall, blood rushing in her ears, running into the first open door and slamming it behind herself. It was some kind of bedroom. Just her luck; there was only one entrance and exit and she couldn’t risk returning to the hall, so she was trapped here for now.

Not under the bed, everyone always checked the bed, but shit, the only other hiding place would be the closet and that one only had one way in and out, same as the room, so if she was discovered there, she would for sure be done for.

Now she just had to wait till whatever entered was on the other side of the room and then book it out again. She could do this.

W-What?

“Do you like the carpet? Leon picked it out, floor was hardwood when it was still my parents’ bedroom, but we both decided we have nightmares that make us fall out of bed way too often for that, even if the upkeep is more complicated.”

Leon, the DSO agent. Was he okay? Wait no he was also her… friend… and weird goofy father figure.

Emily, she needed to save Emily.

Could he? Grace wasn’t sure… Sure, the voice didn’t seem hostile, but Gideon also had a somewhat soft register when he wanted to. Tone didn’t mean anything.
