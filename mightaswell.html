Chapter 1 – leon – Maybe don’t touch that with your bare fingers, yea?

Start notes:

Leon encounters a flesh wall with no mystical properties whatsoever.

Fun fact publishing this with a hangover, just as vendetta leon would have wanted

"Status report."

Leon passed the empty lab with his flashlight again, the repeat still not changing the fact this place was clearly abandoned a long time ago, "Still nothing of note Hunnigan."

The guys higher up found this lab in Arias’ files. And with it being categorised as ‘compromised’ while being located right under Portland of all places (read: large metropolitan area with a great amount of potential casualties), they once again made Leon cancel his vacation to handle what was sure to be a complex crawling with B.O.W.s. Apparently the fact Leon was part of stopping the madman owner wasn’t good enough.

But to everyone’s surprise, when Leon unsealed the latch that led to the lab, there was no zombie horde waiting for him. Shit there was basically nothing waiting for him, just general disarray and years old blood splatters. Which begged the question: what the hell happened here?

Hunnigan sighed in his ear, clearly also done with this mission. “Any luck with the control room or generators at least?”

“Negative. Just storages and labs with enough dust to kill every asthmatic in the continental United States.”

She hummed, “Continue with sweep, then.”

“Roger.”

He’d like to find the generators first, personally. This flashlight only thing was starting to piss him off.

But maybe his luck wasn’t so bad, as he turned a corner he came across a floor plan with actual room descriptions. Finally. Who the hell puts the fire escape plan not into the first room of a building? Okay, so Leon came from here, so this corridor should be over there… So if he turned left here, right here, through this lab and storage… Bingo, generators right next to the server room. How convenient.

He started walking down his planned path when his comms came to life again, “Leon?”

“Hm?”

Hunnigan’s tone was carefully neutral, almost casual, “…I missed your jokes.” ‘I’m glad you’re doing better’.

Leon paused, looking at the suddenly very interesting empty plant pot in one of the corners, “…Acknowledged.”

He has been doing better. Fuck he really has. It was hard to imagine. He tried not thinking too hard about it, personally. On one hand because life has taught him to take whatever wins he could (and him no longer actively yearning to eat a bullet was very much a win) and on the other hand because he could just feel his improvement was dangerously close to the whole pile of unresolved feelings labelled ‘more than platonic feelings about Chris Redfield’, which he would one day get to addressing. Totally.

As he was momentarily lost in thought, he finally came to the large storage, just before the generator room.

Ah. Well if anything was to ‘compromise’ a lab, that would do it.

“Hunnigan I found a giant wall of. Something. Looks organic.”

What little his flashlight illuminated showed a red-black shiny writhing mass, which covered the rather big room from top to bottom, forming a solid wall, though thankfully leaving the door to what was supposed to be the breaker room free.

“Hostiles?”

He walked to the other door slowly, not letting his eyes and flashlight leave the thing, “Negative, but it doesn’t seem like just regular bioweapon wall gunk either. It’s moving for one.”

“Towards you?”

“No just. In place, I guess… Proceeding to next room, hopefully it doesn’t decide to eat me when I come back.”

“Affirmative.”

He walked in, immediately coming face to face with about a million different circuit breakers and buttons.

“C’mon, c’mon, big red lever that says power…” he mumbled to himself idly, before his eyes locked onto one that, while not red, certainly seemed big and important enough to be what he was looking for.

He shrugged with a hum, putting the flashlight between his shoulder and cheek, using both hands to flip the lever. For a moment it seemed to not have an effect, before a large pop sounded behind the metal, followed by the steadily increasing hum of a transformer. The doors behind Leon closed with a hiss, startling him a bit, before finally, the lights flickered on.

Leon clicked off his flashlight, “That shines a bit of a light on the situation.” He activated his comm again, walking to the next room, where a myriad of servers slowly blinked back online, “Hunnigan, I got the power back on and an access point, though with how long ago the power went out I’m not sure how much of the data is not yet in silicon heaven.”

“We’ll see what we can get. Plug in the transmitter.”

Leon got to the centre computer, hitting the power button and starting to fish out the little transmitter fob out of his pants, eyes looking over the small note with a passcode and blood splatters on it. His brows furrowed. Seriously, where were all the bodies? Sure, if the thing that made this place ‘compromised’ was the giant bioweapon flesh wall, there was probably enough time to evacuate everybody, but the mess and the extensive blood splatters told a different story, a story with more mobile B.O.W.s in the starring role.

The computer finally turned on, Leon using the convenient note with a password to unlock it and once on the home screen he unceremoniously plugged the fob in.

“Transmitter in.” Now to let the wonders of modern technology be wondrous.

“Receiving.” Hunnigan paused for a moment, probably parsing through the preliminary technical parameters, “Hm. Big portion of the data is corrupted, but that’s for the techs to figure out. Starting transfer… Estimated time thirty minutes to an hour, better get comfortable.”

Leon sighed, leaning over the keyboard, “I’m gonna see if I didn’t anger my wall friend by turning on the lights.”

“Be careful.”

“Aren’t I always?” he chuckled, walking back into the storage room with his pistol at the ready.

Now fully lit, Leon could properly see just how strangely the thing clung to the walls, vaguely reminding him of that documentary about mushrooms he caught once. It was also way darker in colour than he previously thought: turns out the colour wasn’t that dark because of how dark everything was, but because it was simply more black than red. Despite the weird mold like way it ended, it was still distinctly fleshy, squelching with each little twitch and writhe.

Leon has never seen anything like it before. Which was saying a lot and was also very concerning. There shouldn’t be anything he hasn’t seen. There being something he hasn’t seen means it’s something new. C-virus level new. Which he really didn’t care for.

He took a few steps closer, now only a foot or so away from the main wall, trying to see if he could spot anything more up close, “Still inactive, but I don’t like what I’m seeing here.”

“Anything familiar?”

“No.”

“Shit.”

“Yea.” He poked it a little with the front of his gun. No response.

“There, I found one file that looks like it mentions the experiments they did there… Hm. Something about introducing the A-virus to ‘the prototype’, probably what you’re seeing.”

“Hm. Did our good friends the evil scientists have anything interesting to say about the results of that experiment?”

“’Results unchanged, product still less effective than base components.’”

“Well let’s hope that means that Wally here is the only one of his kind. Anything on how they abandoned this place? Bit too much blood around for the zero bodies.” He poked it a little stronger, still to no response, it simply squelched around his gun and carried on being gross.

“Security footage hasn’t come through yet, but I’ll keep an eye out, in the meantime I’ll try to find out more about what experiments they ran.”

“Alright. Till then.” He clicked his comm off, still nudging the wall with his gun, honestly waiting for a hand or a giant eye to peak out, but nope, nothin’. Just a wall of weird moldy flesh.

Leon walked around the wall, poking it in different places with his gun and foot but it really did just seem perfectly fine being an ominous flesh wall.

And then he made one crucial error.

He poked it with his finger.

A jolt went through him, completely freezing him in place for a moment, before he scrambled back, getting hit with an eery air of dreadful familiarity. He could suddenly even hear the high-pitched whine that haunted his nightmares. Las plagas. Whatever small amount of it remained within him somehow reacted with all its dead might to the small touch of his fingers against the wall.

The wall also flinched in a weird way, its writhing now more active, almost bubbling as it slowly inched forward and towards him. A black tendril shot out of it, which Leon ducked under, “What, no dinner first? Straight to tentacles?”

He reached to reactivate his comm, “Hey Hunnigan, how long till I can extract?”

“What did you do?”

“Touched it.” he shot a tendril and kicked another. Honestly they were pretty slow. Slower than the regular B.O.W.s he faced. Probably what the scientists meant by it being less effective.

She sighed, “Of course you did.”

“Hey, in my defence I was poking it with my shoe for the last 5 minutes and it didn’t seem to mind- Hgk! How was I supposed to know the A-virus inside it would feel the fact I had the worms eight years ago?” He was clearly just wasting ammo, the wall absorbing his bullets like a sponge, so he switched to his knife, cutting the tentacles off as they attempted to swipe at him.

“It reacted to the plaga?”

“Sure felt that way- Fuck!” the cut off tentacles grabbed his feet, holding him in one place. He started cutting away at the webbing like structure holding him down, but it just kept regenerating.

“Leon?! What’s your status?!”

His hand almost got caught in the mass, which suddenly started dragging him further towards the wall, making him lose his balance, landing on the black substance which quickly spread all over him. Fuck, it was doing some weird assimilation bullshit! That’s why there were no bodies, shit, that’s why there weren’t even any plants. Biomass is biomass and if you want to grow into a big strong flesh wall you need a lot of it.

“Leon!” he could barely hear Hunnigan over the persistent screech of the plaga command signal burning against his ears, getting stronger the closer to the main wall he was dragged.

He struggled as best as he could with more and more of his body enveloped by the strange substance, but as his ankles hit the main body of the wall, suddenly the signal spiked and for a frightening moment of clarity it felt like it was (and he knew this sounded crazy the moment he thought it) communicating with him. Telling him it was okay. It was a friend. That he was its friend. And that it would show him something wonderful.

Fuck no. Leon gained a second wind in his struggle, thrashing and twisting against being slowly slid into the wall.

As the weird substance crawled over his face, he wondered if he should maybe stop wearing fingerless gloves, since he works with biohazards.

Something to consider, if nothing else.

Chapter 2 – leon – The economy is still in shambles.

Start notes:

Think fast chuckle nuts! (throws leon’s biggest trauma into his face casually)

Leon woke up. That was the first surprise. Because last he remembered a B.O.W. pulled him into itself.

Second surprise was that he was lying… outside? He felt a steady breeze against his nose and was that grass under him? He opened his eyes slowly, only to be met with a starry sky and the tops of trees in his vision.

“What the…” Leon sat up and looked around, fuck he felt weird. Kinda akin to being hung over. He privately chuckled, “Never drinking that again.” He was sitting in a strange patch of black moss in a ditch next to a road. It was not too cold, not too hot, and the breeze was decently warm. He put a hand to his comm but only got static back. Disconnected. Great.

So. Straight up. He should address the elephant in the room. Or. The elephant in the ditch with him: He was outside in the middle of nowhere in pretty warm weather, when last time he checked he was in an evil Portland basement during a relatively cold November. What the fuck.

Well. Several ways this could have happened: A) He got rescued, got out safe and sound, went on a different mission where he pulled a Chris, got a TBI and forgot a few things. Plausible option, if it wasn’t for the fact he was still wearing the exact same dirty clothes he had on before and the fact nobody would waste resources on rescuing him. B) Somehow Wally spat him out on the other side of the country with better weather, and C) He’s hallucinating. As much as that option made his stomach lurch uncomfortably, he’s had B.O.W. induced hallucinations before. Wouldn’t you know it, they even involved the same parasite that made the wall react the way it did now.

Leon groaned as he got up on weirdly shaky legs, “Great. Parasite hallucinations. Never thought I’d have to deal with that again.” Though he was still mostly thinking about options A and B, because from what he remembered of the plaga hallucinations, they were very distinctly dream-like, while this was regular reality as far as he could tell. Aside from being a bit loopy and his head hurting a little, everything else seemed to be perfectly clear.

He walked up to the road, looking both ways, before settling on walking in the direction of the halo of light pollution. His top priority was to somehow get in contact with HQ after all, so heading towards civilization was still his best bet. Poor Hunnigan, he doesn’t like scaring her like this and be it teleportation or amnesia, his money was on the fact he was very abruptly disconnected, giving her yet another small heart attack. Her insurance better be great, her cardiologist had to be making bank and it better not be on her dime.

But honestly, this was very on brand for him and his shit luck: Stuck in the middle of nowhere, no phone(after he somehow managed to lose his 6th he decided he can just check any missed calls after missions) and no comms, eh, well at least he wasn’t cold and had decent bit of cash on him. Small victories.

He walked for maybe 5 minutes before coming across a gas station, “Hm. Mizoil. Nothing good ever happens on a Mizoil.” He glanced at the board with the gas prices, “Well the economy didn’t improve since the last time I checked.”

Should he make a pitstop? Probably not. Gas station alcohol was always so damn overpriced.

Leon has been cutting back, mostly because any time he started drinking alone he thought of that worried little frown Chris shot him when he pulled out his flask while checking in on him at the medbay after Arias, but in Leon’s humble opinion this situation made a little drink to take the edge off warranted.

Ah well.

He pulled out the aforementioned flask and took a swig, the burn a familiar comfort in the face of all this bullshit. It was almost empty, maybe he should make that pitst-

Leon barely noticed the clatter of his flask against asphalt, vision tunnelling, only taking in the sign in front of him, each letter making his heart pound in his ears more and more.

‘WELCOME TO RACCOON CITY’

He took an unconscious step back. No. Not this place. How? Was this all fake after all?

He chuckled deliriously, “Why the gas prices tho? Stupid ass detail to get wrong don’t you think, Wally?” he fell on his ass, his knees giving out, and shit the ground under him felt so real, too, but no way he was here, that was- it couldn’t be.

His breathing was picking up speed, that was probably not good. This was a crisis situation, he needed to get a grip. Stay calm. Didn’t they beat his panic attacks out of him years ago? Fuck. Fuck fuck fuck, no, not this place, not here, this isn’t real. Yea. This wasn’t real. It was stupid fucking plaga hallucination. Option C all the way, why not, who gave a fuck that the asphalt was digging into Leon’s hand uncomfortably; that the pain he felt from tugging at his hair was clear and sharp and that his plaga has been dead for almost a decade. This had to be fake. This was meant to get a rise out of him. He was fine. He was on the job. He was always fine while on the job. He totally wasn’t getting flashes of the faces he failed, faces that never made it out because he just wasn’t good enough-

He reached blindly for the flask that was somewhere in front of him, taking swig after swig until it was completely empty. He could feel the static in his head muffle, replaced with the slight buzz of alcohol. Good. Fuck. It was fine. He was fine.

He slowly started getting up. Now that he was a little calmer, he could see the tagline was… different. Whereas in his nightmares he was always mocked by ‘Home of Umbrella’, the sign before him read ‘Home of the Green Herb’.

He walked closer, hand ghosting over the metal, “Someone painted over this…” he could still see the little indents of the Umbrella logo under the slightly off shade green covering it.

Leon picked up his walking pace unconsciously, putting distance between him and the sign. Okay what the fuck was happening here. This felt real. This felt incredibly real. Just as real as the cold of the lab and the strange texture of the tendrils that dragged him into the moldy flesh wall. He truly didn’t believe he was dreaming this. But that meant what? Fucking time travel? Clearly not, based on the gas prices. He wheezed hysterically, so what, dimensional travel? What was this, a Star Trek episode? …Was there dimensional travel in Star Trek? He’s never actually seen it. Point was no matter how he spun it, he seemed to be in an entirely impossible scenario: either stranded in a city which has been bombed for the last 16 years or having an extremely realistic hallucination.

The hallucination option was the more believable one, but there was just this nagging in the back of Leon’s mind that told him this was real. Maybe he should have tried breathing through his little panic attack instead of chasing it with alcohol, keep his head clear. But believing his gut, even while a few bottles deep had yet to fail him, so who knew.

Okay, game plan: treat this as if it were real for the time being. Wouldn’t want to jump off a cliff thinking he could fly only to break his legs by the fall. Treating this as real with a healthy dose of scepticism was the safest approach.

Next step: go into town and either the scale of what the illusion would need to get right would fall apart with inconsistencies and then hopefully Leon would be let go, or it would be confirmed as real at which point Leon would… Well he would cross that bridge when he got to it.

Perfect plan. Perfectly okay and normal plan. The soft breeze made the tear tracks on Leon’s face sting, “Stupid fucking B.O.W.s with their stupid fucking tricks.” He grumbled under his breath as he hastily wiped his eyes. One fucking town sign and he was that baby-faced rookie having a panic attack in a dingy motel room with a dirty bullet wound in his shoulder again. Nowadays crying was an at-home activity, goddammit.

A sort of uncomfortable static spread through his head as he walked, the pause in active plan making giving room for the flashes to return, now not just of the zombies in police uniforms but of little Sherry crying out for him as they got manhandled by men in heavy gear; of the ominous stack of papers that signed his freedom away…

If he didn’t think about it it would be fine. This was just part of the mission. He’s been trained in being actually tortured, a few flashbacks were light work.

He paused as he came out of the forest to a road which overlooked the city, “Light work…”

The city was a bright beacon of life despite the clear late hour, the skyline was a little different than Leon remembered, but to be fair he didn’t exactly spend a lot of his time studying it when he had the chance and since then his skyline has been rather explosively revamped. He found himself a little mesmerised, it just seemed so alive, small lights of cars driving on streets, an ambulance siren was heard somewhere in the distance; by all accounts it was what a city of this size should look if there never was a mass virus outbreak that ended in a bombing.

Leon finally made it to the city outskirts, passing a few people and cars on the way. It was all so uncanny. Just random young people going from a bar, a homeless guy sleeping on a bus stop, dark store fronts with reasonable names, hours and contact info on the doors, too normal and too detailed to be pieced together by some bioweapon.

He walked aimlessly some more, before halting in front of a digital clock that was next to one of the bus stops. Because it showed him the date.

“June 17th 2006? What?” That was such a random day, and to his knowledge nothing significant happened on it.

While the hallucination idea seemed the most logically plausible so far, what would be the goal of showing him an unbombed Raccoon City? Torturing him with what could have been? Okay, plausible, but why go to all this length and detail? And how would it achieve this much detail? Yes, Leon vaguely recognised these streets but he for sure didn’t remember what number busses were on which stop. And why 2006? Wouldn’t it have been more optimal to show him current day? Hell, make him wake up at his would-be desk, make him meet the coworkers he never got to meet and then see them get turned into zombies in front of his eyes, why the preamble of the 3am forest walk?

As much as he hated to admit it, his gut feeling seemed to be correct and, some-fucking-how, Wally transported him to some kind of alternate Raccoon City which didn’t get bombed in 1998. Would explain the same gas prices too, wouldn’t it. There was an energy crisis around that time. And a recession right around the corner.

Okay so. Sure. Alternate universe. Why not. Seemed like the kind of thing to happen to him. He dragged a hand across his face with a tired sigh. Obviously next step was figuring out how to get back, but… He glanced at the clock again. Probably not a lot of ways to snoop out any bioweapon activity at half past 3 in the morning in a seemingly regular city. And admittedly Leon was very curious as to what differences were there between his own universe and this one: where this version of Raccoon City stayed unbombed and uninfected.

Well for that kind of snooping he’d need to talk to someone local.

He scanned the street again, eyes settling on a modestly sized neon sign reading ’24 hour bar’.

“Bingo.”

Chapter 3 – leon – Mr. Classified

Start notes:

Leon gets depressed and has an alcoholism about it. So same old same old.

The bar was honestly cleaner than Leon expected.

Hell, it even had a no smoking sign, he knew just how rare that was in the early 2000s. Overall it looked like he couldn’t have chosen a more pleasant place to do his snooping at. Of course it would all depend on just how talkative could a bartender of a 24-hour bar be at 3am, but Leon’s hopes were high as he walked through the entry hall.

He opened the heavy door and walked in, being greeted by a small room with several tables along the back and left side, only the corner one was occupied by 3 men playing some kind of card game, one of them shot Leon an unreadable look, to which Leon nodded politely, making the man mind his own business again.

The right side had a thick TV on a high up shelf (it made Leon get a little nostalgic, government work had him spoiled, most TVs he saw these days were flat) and a doorway leading to another room, probably where the bar was. Soft music came through a crackling grey computer speaker placed next to the TV, just loud enough to provide pleasant background noise, but not so loud it would interrupt any conversations. Cozy.

Leon casually walked through the doorway, this room was long and relatively thin, left side having some more tables while the bar was on the right, with some kind of either pool or a foosball table all the way in the back, Leon couldn’t tell from his spot.

The bartender was a dark skinned guy around his age, with hair in small braids that reached to about his shoulders and casual clothes. Leon’s eyes briefly caught on the small rainbow flag that was taped to one of the taps in front of the man. Well shit, whatdoyaknow, a gay friendly bar. Leon’s luck seemed to be better than he thought. Or, well, it was June apparently, probably just seasonal decoration, as it were. Was pride month a thing in 2006? He was too owned by the government at that time to remember.

The bartender noticed his little pause, looking at him with calculating eyes, seemingly daring him to say anything about the little flag. Leon just slid into one of the bar stools, with a casual: “Evening. Or heh, morning I guess.”

The bartender sighed seemingly in relief as he greeted back, “Morning. What can I get you?”

“Anything you’d recommend?”

“Hm. You strike me as a whiskey or vodka guy.”

“Guilty.”

“Visiting?”

“You could say that.”

“Well then I can recommend Mr. Raccoon’s scarf, if you’re feeling touristy.”

Leon waved a hand, “Sure.”

As the bartender started mixing 2 types of vodka and some random flavourings and mixers, Leon checked his wallet (which somehow never got lost despite it being in the same pocket as his phone). This would get real awkward real fast if he didn’t have any pre 2006 notes on him. Okay, a 2005 fifty, great. And a bunch of others too. He quickly went through all his finances, hopefully not looking too suspicious while at it and took all the post 2006 money and put it in one of his hidden pockets, same one he kept his IDs in. Wouldn’t want future money to just be discovered on him with a regular pat down. Not that he planned on getting patted down but better safe than sorry.

Just in time for the bartender to place the finished drink on a coaster in front of him with a smooth “That will be six dollars.”

Leon handed over the money and took a sip, not bad, strange flavour profile but not unpleasant, “Mr. Raccoon knows what’s up.” he nodded appreciatively.

“He has been a hit with both locals and visitors so far.”

There was a pause, in which Leon considered his options; possible conversation starters, weird to just start asking a random barman about the town history, right? But thankfully the bartender was seemingly bored, and a stranger was just the right amount of interesting, “So. Not from around here. Kind of visiting… You wouldn’t happen to be kind of visiting family, would you?”

Strange thing to ask, though if most of the economy was still based on pharmaceutical exports, Leon admittedly didn’t exactly look like he would be here for that, “Nope, just. Passing through, really.”
