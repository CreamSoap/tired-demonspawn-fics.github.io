Despite what the movies and books would tell you, being a werewolf didn't mean you only transformed during the full moon.

Actually, despite what any rational person would think and tell you, being a werewolf was, in Stanley's humble opinion, very useful at times. No better way to mask your tracks than when the tracks literally cease to be yours. Who would waste time tracking a large wolf when you're looking for the size 13s of the idiot who owes you money.

Of course, for all those swift getaways he first had to learn how to control that transformation, but when you're in the trunk of someone’s car you suddenly find yourself a master of making your mouth capable of chewing through leather and plastic. Werewolf or no werewolf.

The whole deal didn’t even have what could be described as outright downsides! Well except for one small elephant in the room…

Yeah, when in wolf form Stanley gets side tracked more easily, a stray smell or twig snap making his mind cloud (especially at those times where he had gone for weeks without a proper meal, which, now that he thinks about it, is almost all the time these days) and he’s more likely to pounce at a sound rather than hide and try to see what it is, but now he's got a get out of a hospital visit free card with all physical wounds healing when he transforms, only leaving scars behind if it’s a big enough wound.

Also, his natural hearing, smell and physical strength are enhanced even in his human form. (Oh, and how many situations did that help him get out of)

The only small, tiny, little downside, the aforementioned elephant, was the full moon.

With it, one was suddenly intimately reminded of why lycanthropy is considered a curse.

Control over when and how he transforms? Nope. Control over his own body while transformed? Nuh-uh. Recollection of anything he did while transformed? Oh, how he wishes for that one.

The only thing he recalls feeling from the whole experience is a hungry fog, very much like the one he sometimes experiences during a regular turn, but much more intense.

He's next to his car overlooking the sunset and next thing he knows he's in some woods outside of a gas station 2 miles (if he’s lucky) away from where he was, and he has mystery blood all over his mouth and hands that he prays isn't human.

Only upside to the full moon turning is that, much like with his normal transformation, his clothes are alright after the fact, pocket content and all.

Stanley would've probably started looking into cures or whatever ages ago if his turning left him naked.

Getting new clothes every time would bankrupt him more than he already is. Not to mention all the times transformations would catch him unawares in the early days.

It would’ve cost him his car keys, his wallet… oh one shutters to imagine.

Well at least the walk back to the Stanleymobile is more annoying than anything. An inconvenience at most even before he could control his transformation and just ran those 2 miles in like 2 minutes instead of walking them in 45.

Plus, sometimes he comes out of the fog still as a wolf, so he didn’t even need to waste time and energy transforming back to a beast.

But that's all just silver lining. All in all, Stanley hates the full moons. The not knowing, the lack of control, the disorientation after the fact and the fear of ‘what if this time it’s not deer blood’ is unbearable.

And worst of all it gets him in situations like this.

It was the summer of ’80 and, freshly banned from Nevada, Stanley flipped a coin to see which direction on the 95 should he head in; that’s how he ended up in the Oregon woods in the middle of nowhere. Which suited him just fine, the less civilised the less the chance of the now expected mystery blood to be of morally and mentally scarring origins.

Stan parked his car in a small clearing which only had a tiny unkept dirt road leading to it, sat on a tree stump just barely in sight of the impromptu parking lot and waited.

He found out early on that fighting the full moon only resulted in waking up 5 towns over and news reports of a huge beast sighting on the radio, so when he started feeling the familiar tug of the moon, only a resigned sigh and a “Here we go.” left him.

What he didn’t expect was to wake up in a cage on a leash with a muzzle.

Oh great, my luck finally turned. He thought. Stan was sort of waiting for this day honestly, being a 10-foot wolf and all, it was a wonder he hadn’t been caught sooner by some hunter or other.

Not that he was all that panicked, everything about his current predicament could be solved fairly easily. By turning back, the muzzle, the collar, hell even the bars of the cage would become useless, being comically large for a normal human.

Maybe luck was on his side yet. Now just to turn back.

…

I can’t turn back.

That’s when he noticed weird runes giving off an ambient sort of glow carved on the chain holding him, presumably also being on the muzzle and collar.

Oh no.

Stanley tried (and failed), to cut the chains with his claws but he barely left even a small scratch. He tried loosening the muzzle, after all his jaw strength was much stronger and was sure to bite through the chains, magic or not, but came to similar level of success.

Damn these stupid paws without opposable thumbs! If I had normal hands and a bobby pin this would be a sinch.

And to be fair he had several pins of all shapes and sizes in his coat, his sleeves, hell even some in his boots. Which were all currently probably in some weird dimension or wherever did his clothes go when he transformed.

In his mounting panic he must’ve made more noise than he realised, because he heard a door open and some feet scurrying down a flight of stairs. That’s when he started to even consider the world outside the cage.

It was some sort of log cabin, bare bones in the furnishing department, but with scrapes and such on the floor. Clearly it was all moved out in a rush, presumably to make space for his cage.

The only furnishing remaining was a table with a lamp, some metal scraps and possibly hundreds of books that covered both the desk and the floor along with the scraps.

Clearly not a hunter then, what sort of hunter would have a book on… He squinted. Theoretical physics in regard to cryptobiology? Heh. That sounds like some nerd shit Ford would- don’t think about him right now… Okay a researcher? God fucking dammit that’s worse. Well maybe they won’t dissect me, think positively. Could’ve probably shot me dead if they have the means to keep me contained. So. Therefore, right? Therefore, they want to keep me alive. Right. Okay.

Not that Stanley had been shot before while turned, at least as far as he could remember. Who knew what his full-moon self got up to.

Plus wasn’t there supposed to be some deal with silver and werewolves? Maybe he should’ve tried to study his affliction more, aside from personal trial and error. After all, if werewolves were real some of the slop written about them was bound to not be completely bogus.

Finally, the door to the room opened. Stan quickly pretended to be asleep. Peering through almost closed eyelids.

In came a lanky man in roughly his early 30s with light brown hair, round spectacles perched on top of a rather large nose and a lab coat. Stan’s intuition was right, a nerd to the max.

The man readjusted his glasses “I could have sworn I heard some rustlin’ in here…” he mumbled to himself, a surprising southern accent making itself known.

The mystery man stopped at the desk, probably jotting down something, it was hard to see with the wall of books covering the actual desk top, not to mention the trying-to-fake-sleep. He finished writing whatever it was and then stepped a little closer to Stan’s cage.

“Well aren't ya a cute little feller, when ya're not tryin' to bite people’s heads off.” He smiled slightly.

“Must've bin' real plumb tired, didn't ya. Or maybe the anaesthesia was stronger than we thought…”

Than WE thought? Goddammit, of course one nerd couldn’t take me down on his own.

“No, for a beast that large 'n that… active, ya should be up 'n tryin' to chew 'n slash your way out of here yesterday.”

Clearly a think-out-loud type of guy. And he thinks I’m cute, let’s hope that translates to him not dissecting me. Fuckin’ hell, here goes nothing.

Stan opened his eyes properly, not moving anything else, and started tracking the scientist as he walked around the room, talking to himself, occasionally thoughtfully slapping his hands against his body.

“Not the type to hibernate, are ya? I mean it is summer, so probably not, but one can never know with ya anomalous fellers. ‘N it’s bin’ a full day, ya should be awake by now, surely.” He picked up a notebook and started listing through it and then finally briefly made eye contact with Stan.

The man did a double take. Stan blinked at him, unimpressed. This will be a long day. Hopefully no one breaks into the Stanleymobile while he’s stuck here.

“Oh! You’re awake!” The man sounded nervous now, apparently locking eyes with a 10-foot tall predator will do that to you. “A-An’ a lot calmer than two days ago. Probably an aftereffect of ‘em sedatives…” He jotted something else down.

Apparently, Stan has spent an entire day and night asleep. Well the welds on the cage looked pretty fresh, so that’s not that surprising. His worry for the Stanleymobile went up though, his entire existence was in that car.

“Ah well-“, he cleared his throat, “If y’all excuse me.” The man took the notebook with him and left the room.

Polite to a beast, how nice.

The giant wolf will be a breakthrough, Stanford was sure of it!

Two days ago now, he and Fiddleford were on the hunt for the multibear, when it attacked them. And what a majestic beast it was… Save the biting, snarling and general unpleasant disposition. And to think he has not yet heard of such a beast stalking the woods! That means it has migrated here recently!

What an exiting find for Ford’s Grand Unified Theory of Weirdness.

Thank Moses they already had gear to fight a giant monster with them, the mystical cuffs he bartered from the fairies were practically made for this! It’s as if the universe has finally heard Stanford’s cries.

He made Fiddleford slightly enlarge their prepared multibear-sized cage, keeping the beast sedated throughout the day.

Oh, how marvellous it all was. The wolf was so much and yet so little like a normal wolf.

One’s common sense would dictate that if it looks like a giant wolf, growls like a giant wolf and tries to bite your head off like a giant wolf, then you’re probably just dealing with a giant wolf. However, upon tranquilising it and looking just a tad closer, Ford’s excitement was rising steadily through the roof.

The chest was wrong, the bone structure of the front and back paws didn’t match with any conventional wolf species, its ears were a tad lower than they ought to, it even had about 10 less teeth than it should for crying out loud!

Everything pointed towards the wolf being naturally occurring rather than the result of an incident with those height altering crystals Ford found earlier that year.

He should really get around to cataloguing those…

Oh how he felt like a little schoolgirl with a crush whenever a new discovery had been made.

Ford’s coffeepot musings were interrupted by Fiddleford coming from the research and development room, which they cleared out for the cage.

“Well aren’t ya grinnin’ like a possum eatin’ a sweet tater. The coffee ain’t that good.”

“On the contrary, right now it tastes like victory! Oh that beast is truly amazing, I can feel that we are on the edge of a breakthrough, my friend.”

“Don’t I got great news for ya then, our little beastie is up.”

Ford took his cup of coffee “Really? After our last encounter I would think it would fight more… audibly.”

“I thought so too, but the feller ain’t makin’ a peep. My hypothesis is there’s still residual tranquiliser that has a calmin’ effect on it.” They fell into step, heading in the direction of the R&D room.

“Hm. Unlikely. For its weight and most likely speed of metabolism based on similar species, the last of it should’ve worn of 2 hours ago at the latest.”

“That's what's weird about it ain’t it? No youngins hangin' around, likely no territory since we have bin' through that neck of the woods before 'n have not bin' attacked. So why now? 'N why such a sudden change in temperament? Could it be ‘em fairy collar ‘n chain?” They walked through the door.

“Not likely, those are only supposed to passively collect ambient magic from the atmosphere, making them harder to destroy. Or at least that’s what I gathered from the explanation I was given…”

The beast bristled in the cage, clinking its chains in the process. It looked at Ford intently. Its stance no longer relaxed but coiled up. However not posed to strike. Like it was startled, afraid. But that’s just ridiculous, which one of them is the 3-meter beast and who’s the not even 2-meter humans.

“Ah shucks, maybe we spoke too soon, looks like it's wide awake now. 'N stressed to high heavens to boot. Poor feller.”

“Well, if all goes according to plan, we can release it again soon.” If Ford was honest the beast’s eyes were freaking him out a little bit, especially since they were locked on him, not even a glance at Fiddleford. Probably because it already met him in a nonhostile scenario?

“How is that modified scanner coming along?”

“Almost done, still workin' out some of the kinks but it works.” He reached into the desk drawer and pulled out a car battery, then picked up the device in question, which until then laid on the desk, and started hooking it up to the battery. “It can only record readings so far, to git 'em out y’all have to use the doohickies in the basement.”

Ford sighed “How many times will I have to ask you to call the spectrogram and oscilloscope by their proper names before you do it?”

“I'll start callin 'em that when doohickies stops bein' more convenient to say.” The beast let out a weird snort-like sound. Fiddleford handed Ford the prepped device, their fingers brushing briefly. “See, the big feller agrees with me.”

Ford went around the cage scanning the creature, who still maintained eye contact with him, although has since come down from its spring-like stance and sat down.

“I highly doubt it can agree with anything.” He paused and turned to the beast, “Can you?” The wolf blinked owlishly back at him.

Ford shrugged and finished his circle around the cage as the device gave a ‘scan successful’ message on its small display.

Ford held his chin in thought, the beast’s eyes still not leaving him.

“It is rather calm, isn’t it? But there weren’t lesions of any kind anywhere on it, no blood on its snout or claws– so we didn’t interrupt a mealtime, and if it were still hungry it wouldn’t be so calm at present.”

Fiddleford shrugged, sitting at the desk and toying with his Cubic’s cube, “Emma-May ‘n Tater are almost settled down downtown, we could ask her. She is the one with the doctorate in biology out of the three of us, Stanford.”

Ford grumbled, his great mood suddenly soured, “Fiddleford you know full-well that your wife cannot stand to be in my presence.”

“Ya're makin' a mountain out of a mole hill, buttercup, y’all be fine. Y'all talk about science 'n she'll talk about science 'n y’all both be just tickled pink by it all in the end.”

Fiddleford always did this, ‘I wanna make my best friend and wife git along, is that such a crime?’ Yes. Yes it was. At least in Stanford’s very humble opinion.

Their quote unquote rivalry began out of nowhere, at least by Ford’s recounting. They were fine the first few times they met but overtime her opinion of him somewhat… worsened. Ford has tried many times to pick an incident that would spark the vitriol the woman had for him but found none.

The best answer he got to was some petty thing about keeping her and Fiddleford apart, but that’s ridiculous, surely as a woman of science herself she understands the importance of the research him and Fiddleford are engaging in.

Ridiculous, absolutely ridiculous the rivalry at play.

Also, he was working on a biology based PhD! It was just taking longer than predicted.

The wolf ‘snorted’ again.

“UGH! We are not talking to your wife about this!” he unhooked the scanner from the car battery as the beast made a repeated sort of rumbling sound with its chest.

“I am going to look at these readings, I feel like I’m being mocked here…”

Fiddleford showed unrestrained glee, “Aw, I’m sure Wolfie didn’t mean it.”

“You know full well I wasn’t talking about the dog, buddy!” Ford resumed his petulant march to the exit.

“By the way, the radio said it will start rainin' like a cow pissin’ on a flat rock sometime this week, might wanna go track Wolfie's tracks while we still can.”

Ford stopped, halfway up the small staircase leading to the rest of the house. “I’ll go while the readings are being processed.”

“Ya want company?”

“No… Thank you Fiddleford.” And he left.

The beast was still making that weird repeating sound. If he were less mad right now Ford might’ve even entertained the possibility of it sounding like a laugh.

“And we are not calling it Wolfie!” he called behind himself.

Stupidly good-hearted, well-meaning, adorable Fiddleford and his stupid biologist wife.

“And the stupid weather and the stupid time-sensitive tracking!” Ford yelled to the forest. It didn’t reply.

Ford finally made it to where the beast originally attacked them. He really should’ve brought Fiddleford with him, he was always better at tracking things, but he just had to have that stupid argument about his stupid wife which made Ford reject his all too kind offer.

It was fine, the scans were being processed right now, he had a nice calm beast right in his home, this was still absolutely perfect. Ford’s earlier giddiness returned steadily.

How expensive would it be to build an in-home x-ray? The bone structure must be fascinating, maybe it would finally tell Ford just what kind of mutation Wolfie was. Oh and now he called it Wolfie subconsciously, it’s over. That’s its name now. Maybe he could allow that concession for being ‘hissy’ at Fiddleford earlier.

Thankfully his target was a 3-meter-tall canine and not a little gnome or something, so it left quite an obvious trail in the undergrowth.

He followed the new path of snapped branches and trampled moss and grass until its sudden end near a lone tree stump.

Theories immediately started to surface in Ford’s head. Did it fall down from somewhere? Was it teleported here? That would explain the aggression at least. Unfamiliar land unfamiliar smells, sights, unfamiliar everything. But why wouldn’t Wolfie be disturbed by waking up in their house? In a cage no less. Maybe the energy needed to teleport something of that magnitude had physical side effects? So, it gave Wolfie an upset stomach, so to speak.

Looking around, he barely caught a sight of something red poking through the trees. He’ll stash the teleport theory for later, damn, he should’ve brought the scanner with him. Check for any weird energies, although there were no disturbances in any of his readings two days ago as far as he knew. Just the typical full moon interference.

It was just a small car lot, or well that’s what the clearing was used as right now anyway. Huh, a 1965 El Diablo.

Ford’s mood once again took a turn for the worse at the thought of his brother, though to be fair that is a startlingly similar shade of red to his brother’s car, at least as far as memory serves. It seems to have a custom licence plate also, huh what are the odds.

Ford snorted in irritation. Why is it that all red El Diablo owners have to have a custom licence plate. What does this marvel of wasted money say anyway?

Oh.

Oh no.

“What are the odds indeed…” Stanford muttered to himself.

The very human tracks that led from the car to the tree stump went unnoticed.

It was a quiet crisp autumn morning.

A woodpecker could be heard happily doing its thing, the breeze was creating a pleasant hum running through the trees and the gnomes were plotting revenge against that damned hellhound that messed up their formation training a few months ago.

On a small mountain path 3 men were going on a hike, or at least that’s what one of them thought, the other two had wildly different expectations for this incursion.

The one in the front wouldn’t dare call this a hike, they were on the edge of discovery for crying out loud!

The one sharing his face was slowly considering fratricide.

“What I don’t get, fuckin’ hell- is why I couldn’t just shift and run up the mountain with you nerds like- fuck me- on my back. God fucking dammit, Sixer I think I left my lungs back at your house.” He complained between wheezes.

“Well, Stanley, maybe I think you should get a little more in shape, you can’t always rely on being a werewolf for locomotion.”

“I can and I will, actually.”

The man in the middle, acting as a de facto barrier so a physical fight wouldn’t break out, considered his options: on one hand he intellectually sided with the man ahead of him, Stanley’s ability to change shape could be taken away, they even knew of a way first hand.

On the other hand, he also felt that his lungs left him a while ago, he was simply quieter about it.

“Oh quit your complaining, we’re here anyway.”

They stood in front of a cave entrance. There was a sort of eery atmosphere around, one that even someone not entirely familiar with the supernatural could feel.

It made Stanley a little sick to his stomach.

Though that may have just been the physical exertion.

“Poindexter, I don’t like this place, I don’t think we should uuh- be here. And that’s coming from me. I love trespassing.”

That didn’t seem to detract from the twin’s excitement, if anything his grin was wide now.

“How fascinating! Your supernatural nature is reacting to this place. That confirms my hypothesis of why this place is considered forbidden by the forest dwellers.”

“They got the right idea if you ask me.”

“Aside from the plumb spooky atmosphere, why are we really here, Stanford?” The man with the southern twang to his voice finally spoke up.

“I was just getting to that. So! As you know, I was tracking the manotaurs the other day, trying to get to their hideout to possibly interview them, but I must have gotten turned around because I found this!”

Stanley wasn’t impressed.

“A cave with spooky vibes. Well done.”

“Not just A cave with spooky vibes, THE cave with spooky vibes. I only got a peak, but this could be the answer to everything.”

They all moved into the cave, Ford pulling out a lantern to give them some light.

The cave walls were covered with ancient symbols, runes and cave paintings, the most eye catching of which shared a similar motif: a triangle with arms, legs and a singular eye.

“I’ve only managed to translate a few of the runes, but already they speak of a powerful god of knowledge, a being with answers.”

“I dunno Stanford, all that sounds a lil too good to be true, don’t it?”

“Yeah I’m with Fiddles on this one. This place makes me feel like I’m being watched.”

Ford didn’t listen to them, “Look here, I believe this might be a way of summoning him… huh as well as a warning not too, would you look at that.”

“Well that was fucking stupid of them.” Stanley leaned down to take a better look at one of the paintings, “That’s basically asking some poor schmuck to summon it. ‘Oh yeah here is exactly how to summon this fuckin’ demon, don’t do it by the way, wink wink.’”

Stanford scoffed, “Don’t be ridiculous Stan, clearly these people were simply unprepared for a being of such magnitude. You agree, right Fiddleford?”

He didn’t look like he did.

“Maybe we should explore the rest of the cave before summonin' any ancient knowledge demons?” he settled on, diplomatically.

Both brothers grumbled at that, but relented.

‘Maybe this will convince him.’, they thought.

The 3 spent the rest of the morning cataloguing the cave paintings and deciphering the writings to the best of their collective ability, even Stanley pitching in with some of his frankly superior pattern seeking ability.

“Welp, that should be all of 'em I reckon.” Fiddleford got up, popping a crick in his back.

Stanley pushed himself off of the wall he was leaning on, “Fuckin’ finally! Let’s go Sixer!”

“Just a moment! I’ll gather all my things and be right behind you.”

He started collecting all the stray papers around and filing them into his journal.

He hesitated when he got to the page with the translated summoning phrase.

…It couldn’t hurt to try, could it?

He looked behind himself, the light of his companions growing more distant by the second.

His brother did have advanced hearing but…

“Triangulum, entangulum. Meteforis dominus ventium. Meteforis venetisarium.”

…

Hm. Nothing.

Ah well, worth a shot.

He ran to catch up with the rest of their little group, who at this point were complaining about the sun being too sharp after so long in a dark cave.

What none of them had the chance to witness, on account of them being in the middle of the woods, was a fascinating phenomenon that happened at that moment, where all the stoplights of the world turned yellow at the same time.

If someone looked at them too closely, they could faintly hear a shrill, manic laugh deep within themselves.

The birch trees surrounding the cave opened their eyes for the first time in millennia.

They watched the three men with glee.
