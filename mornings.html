Chapter 1 – Robotnik – the morning after

Dr. Ivo Robotnik hated these ‘morale-boosting’ parties.

They were only a cheap excuse for the dim-witted mouth-breathers to satiate their lower urges of being loud and obnoxious then subsequently blame it on the alcohol, as well as the ones who consider themselves ‘cunning’ and ‘resourceful’ to get their higher ups to spill precious ‘state secrets’ and proceed to think themselves the smartest person in the room for learning something that isn’t even under half a dozen passwords. What amateur hour.

Ah well, at least there were some small upsides to the whole thing, seeing how the wannabe-spy’s face crumples when Robotnik rejects their toast, and even better: the frustration after indulging in one or five of them and still not letting any ‘useful’ information slip? Priceless.

Almost makes the whole endeavour worth it.

Of course, the main thing that always gets him through these forced social gatherings is his trusty agent. Sometimes both metaphorically and physically: like when he entertains one would-be-spy too many and must be covertly held up by Stone in order to maintain his dignity while leaving.

Yes, he leads him back to the mobile lab, takes him to his bed, makes sure he doesn’t, at the very least, sleep in his shoes and coat, leaves him a glass of water and sets his alarm for the morning like the loyal lapdog he is.

Which is why Robotnik was rather confused by waking up:

  • on his own, without an alarm
  • with no glass of water in sight

And, most alarmingly:

  • entirely naked.

He was above this! Or at least he damn well thought so before this very morning!

And he was in his lab too! It’s a wonder the security system didn’t obliterate whatever drunken wretch actually managed to bed him. And where was Stone in all of this?! This whole thing is a situation he can’t imagine his barnacle leaving him in.

Just who did he sleep with anyway? It seems this night was just full of disappointments for Robotnik’s self-image: not only did he get drunk enough to sleep with somebody, he got so drunk he didn’t remember the encounter itself!

He sat up, bundling the duvet around his lower half, resting his still somewhat spinning head on his knees. Time to stop feeling sorry for himself and actually figure out if he’ll also need an STI test along with having agent Stone murder someone.

Robotnik raked his eyes slowly around the small bedroom, there wasn’t really much to investigate anyway, with it being designed to be as compact as possible to fit inside the lab: mattress on the ground, a slightly raised platform acting as a nightstand, a small storage space with agent Stone’s shirt draped over it haphazardly, a currently-closed retractable wardro-

…A storage space with WHAT on it?

His eyes snapped back to the shirt, it couldn’t be, could it?

But it was, that small red detailing in the shape of a badnik Robotnik insisted Stone have on the inside of his collar was unmistakable.

It was as if a faint ‘click’ could be heard in his brain as it suddenly decided to bombard him with at least vague flashes of last night. Not ideal but at least it was better than the complete and utter nothing he had been working with.

They got from the party as normal- no wait. That wasn’t right. It all started with that stupid bureaucrat who Robotnik had been entertaining himself with, yes.

She had somehow managed to rope Stone into the lip-loosening ritual as well, which while not having a noticeable effect on him at first did result in both of them having more aerosol than legs.

They were outside the lab, Robotnik still remembers that.

Stone was unlocking it with unusually loopy and unprecise fingers while Robotnik had been talking about something no doubt brilliant while propped up against the wall.

Then, he turned to agent Stone who gave him this… look.

Replaying the uncharacteristically unclear alcohol-soaked memory in his mind’s eye there wasn’t anything special about how Stone stared at him, that’s just how his face looks. Smiling like an idiot, lighting up like a miniature sun. But in that moment, he can remember feeling a deep… want.

And then, like Robotnik’s life was nothing more than a cheap movie, there was a cut in his memory and in the next one they were kissing, making out even. Him. Dr. Ivo Robotnik. And agent Stone.

The rest of his jumbled memories progressed exponentially in their… intensity, let’s say.

Robotnik readjusted himself on the bed nervously. So he slept with agent Stone… Huh.

To be fair that was the most preferable outcome to his investigation, he could recite Stone’s entire medical history from memory, so no worries on that front. Also, their… eloping… would explain why Stone neglected his usual post-party duties.

And, based on his memories, it’s not like he took advantage of Robotnik, as Stone was also quite inebriated (not that he would ever dare to take advantage of him).

Robotnik didn’t like that he was running out of practical, rational things to think about. All that was left was the question of, eugh… feelings.

Better start simple: physical feelings. Yes, he enjoyed himself physically. At least his alcohol drenched memories and how he felt right now supported this. Good. That was easy.

Robotnik groaned into the duvet still around his propped-up knees.

Emotions… he had never been good at emotions.

They were one of his only weaknesses. Not that it mattered most of the time, after all, he had agent Stone to dispatch any emotionally charged outbursts some sub-zero IQ soldier boys inspired in Robotnik. But right now this reliance paradoxically left him blind-sighted, because the emotions were being caused by Stone.

Was Stone attractive? Yes. Gah- curse him, Robotnik was being objective again.

Did he find Stone attractive? That was the more relevant question.

A question that made his traitorous mind bring up visions of last night without him giving it permission to do so: the way Stone touched him so, so carefully, how his face looked when it was full of pleasure, his soft pants of ‘Doctor’- OKAY FINE, yes. Safe to say Robotnik was unfortunately not as above the whole attraction thing as he thought.

Whatever! Add that to the list of disappointing things he learned about himself tonight!

Now for the most frustrating and not clear-cut question: Did this mean anything?

No? Yes? Hard to tell.

Let’s start small: Robotnik didn’t hate agent Stone. In-fact he even tolerated his presence. Actually, he quite enjoyed it. Which is miles above what can be said about any other worthless skintag masquerading as an intelligent being that has unfortunately existed near Robotnik previously.

So what? Physical attraction + not completely despising the man + copious amounts of alcohol = sex? There was something missing in that equation.

A missing link which he didn’t have the time to ponder further, as there came a soft whoosh of the automatic door leading to the room, revealing the man of the hour.

Or the previous night, Robotnik supposed.

“Ah, Doctor, you’re awake!”

Stone looked radiant, glowing, Robotnik had never seen him quite like this. If his normal smiles were miniature suns his entire body now exuded the energy of several supernovas.

He was also noticeably not wearing a shirt, even though the rest of his uniform looked as meticulous as ever, even the tie that simply slung around his neck somehow looked like it was always meant to be worn like that.

Stone looked vaguely down at himself, confused, before perking up, “Oh, right, sorry Doctor, I didn’t have time to sew the buttons back on. But I didn’t want to be entirely out of uniform.”

Robotnik looked away, he didn’t even notice he was staring. Was he staring? Surely not. His eye contact with Stone’s bare chest lasted barely(heh) 2.03 seconds.

He diverted his eyes to the discarded shirt in question, a vague warm feeling of triumph seeping from his drunken memories. Ugh, he ripped it off of Stone, didn’t he? His list of disappointments was starting to become concerningly long. Seeing as it existed at all.

“Your latte, sir.” Stone said, still with that supernova smile and the ever-present glow, handing him a latte in his favourite mug. Robotnik absent-mindedly took a sip, only sparing a single glance at the always perfect latte art: heh, a badnik today.

Stone didn’t bring it up yet. Why hasn’t he brought it up yet? Was he waiting for Robotnik to bring it up?

A chill went down Robotnik’s spine with a stray thought: The latte had a badnik on it. That is as neutral as Stone’s latte art gets. It was safe. It didn’t mean anything. Certainly not a positive opinion of their joint night.

Oh no.

The last part of the equation was slowly completing itself with a sinking realisation.

Stone wasn’t the one who took an advantage here, was it?

Stone’s expression paused a little, before he squatted down next to the bed. A perfectly neutral distance from Robotnik. Not too close, not obviously far. Robotnik messed up. No, he can’t lose his agent like this, with this- this mistake he had little to no control over!

His smile was unchanging, like his namesake, "Do you want to talk about it doctor?"

And find out just how much Stone now despised him? He’s giving him an obvious out for crying out loud!

Robotnik pulled his knees closer, somewhat belatedly realising just how underdressed he was compared to Stone. Not that it mattered, it would seem Stone had seen quite enough of him the previous night, but it made his neck heat up all the same.

Curse his non-perfect and human body.

"...No."

Not even a twitch, just that same, infuriating, radiant smile, though Stone at least paused for a rather noticeable amount of time, "That’s fine too. We don’t have to talk about it if you don’t want to, sir."

It had to be a front. There's no way that- yes, Robotnik was a genius like always, Stone was simply trying to spare his feelings after a night of regret, but... if he can fake this level of... radiance, just how many of those miniature suns were actually black holes in disguise?

Because clearly what happened last night was that a drunk Robotnik came onto the not-so-drunk Stone due to his not yet consciously realised feelings, and Stone, not wanting to risk a disappointed and sexually frustrated Robotnik (and therefore his cushy government job), decided to humour him and is now trying to act like it doesn’t change anything.

He couldn’t look Stone in the eyes, "Fine by me.”

It really wasn’t.

This changed nothing.

This changed everything.

Mostly in the way that it made Robotnik realise one crucial thing:

Agent Stone hated him just like everyone else.

Chapter 2 – Stone – several mornings after

 

Agent Stone knew many things: how to froth all variants of milk perfectly for latte art, how to kill a man and hide the body in under 15 minutes, or even how to stand off to the side in such a way where he is invisible to the meat-headed G.U.N. higher ups, yet always available to his Doctor at meetings.

But the thing he was most pleased with was just how well he has grown to understand one Dr. Ivo Robotnik.

Understanding him was his pride and joy, only falling behind his actual ability to serve the Doctor on his list of most honed and trained skills.

That’s why when the Doctor didn’t want to talk about their encounter the following morning, Stone wasn’t surprised. A tad disappointed maybe, but certainly not surprised. After all, it was one of the 3 or 4 possibilities that immediately popped into his head upon waking up with hazy memories of last night and a sleeping Ivo Robotnik draped across his chest like it belonged to him (which it did, to be fair, as did the rest of him) (the latter being proven accurate rather recently).

As much as the selfish part of him hoped for the option where, upon walking in with the coffee the following morning, the Doctor would demand a ‘round two’ now that they weren’t inebriated, that scenario’s odds weren’t exactly in Stone’s favour.

Because one of the many things the agent has found out about Dr. Robotnik was that he hated change he didn’t explicitly orchestrate.

And, obviously, having drunken sex with your assistant had the risk of bringing on massive, unplanned change, which, again, the Doctor hated, so when Stone eventually carefully extracted himself from the other man, he decided to make this the most normal morning for the Doctor as he could: Stone in uniform and coffee in hand at 8 o’clock sharp.

Well, the uniform wasn’t… all there, but by the end of the day he managed to sew all his buttons back on.

Stone played with the idea of keeping one of them off as a reminder of that breathtaking hunger in the Doctor’s eyes as he impatiently ripped the shirt off him, but ultimately decided against it, as a missing button would break dress code, which was another thing the Doctor despised.

The rest of the day’s energy was… awkward. But again, nothing unexpected. Morning shame was something that Stone did account for after all: hard to maintain normalcy with each sore movement reminding you of last night’s happenings.

Especially when every time Stone would doze off he would be haunted by those angelic, hoarse, pleading, whimpering moans and every time he would close his eyes he could only see the Doctor’s perfect body under him, with that perfect blissed out expression.

If the Doctor was similarly plagued, then no wonder he couldn’t even look Stone in the eyes. Especially when trying to ease into whatever their relationship was developing into.

Or Stone assumed… The following days made him develop… doubts to say the least.

His original assumption was based entirely on Robotnik’s conduct that night:

They were at a party, one of those that the Doctor pretended to hate but always had a great time manipulating drunk people at, when Stone made the error of also getting involved in drinking to the point where, when it came time for their exit, him and Robotnik were mutually supporting each other, instead of their usual one-sided balancing act.

As they came to their lab, Robotnik had been talking about something absolutely brilliant like always (Stone quietly sighed to himself, everything the Doctor said was brilliant. He was brilliant.) and then when Stone turned to him, to extract him from the wall he was leaning on, there was this… shift, as Robotnik suddenly became quiet.

Stone was a bit concerned that the Doctor was about to throw up, but he usually started complaining about being sick long before it became an issue, so that possibility was merely a passing thought in his alcohol-quieted mind.

The lab door whooshed closed behind them when the Doctor suddenly froze, looking at Stone like he was solving a particularly unruly bug in a badnik.

Stone looked back, leaning against the lab wall for support, slowly but surely making a list of tasks he needed to do before leaving, when the Doctor very clumsily sprung into action, his hands reaching out for Stone.

Stone recalls steadying them, making sure they safely landed wherever Doctor’s drunken mind wanted. And if their destination happened to be his face, Stone only had enough presence of mind to hold them there, leaning into their warmth.

He opened his eyes, taking in the dishevelled form of Robotnik before him. He was gorgeous. He always is.

And then, like the simplest thing in the world, with no coherent thought running through Stone’s mind, only an unfiltered feeling of adoration, they both leaned in and kissed.

“Stone. Latte.”

“Of course, Doctor.”

He got lost in the daydream of that night often. But not without some reason: he needed to see if he missed something.

Because the Doctor had become… distant. Almost reverting to how he was when Stone was first assigned to him, but with far less bite.

As he steamed the milk for Doctor’s 17:00 latte Stone kept asking himself the question that has been haunting him for about a week now.

Did the Doctor regret it? Regret him?

Yes, he had been drunk, but he initiated, and he was the one to progress to the next step every time.

Stone would never force him into anything he didn’t want to. Even in the throws of drunken pleasure Stone maintained the main boundary which their relationship had always been based on:

It is not up to Stone how his and Doctor Robotnik’s relationship progressed.

Their entire dynamic was so soaked in this rule that Stone wouldn’t be surprised if it was the fine print in one of the many contracts and NDAs he signed to be stationed under Robotnik.

It might as well have been a law of the universe like gravity or his Doctor’s favourite Euler’s identity with how intrinsic and natural it had become to Stone. So, when there came a big step such as their somewhat accidental encounter, Stone of course left the big decision to the Doctor, as he always did.

So why was their dynamic suddenly interrupted like this?

“Your Latte, sir.”

Robotnik gesticulated vaguely, “Hngn…”

Stone pouted, putting the cup in its spot. No accidental brushing of hands today. Or yesterday. Or ever since that night 2 weeks ago.

For not the first time, Stone truly weighed if he should bring their shared night up as he sat down in his desk chair.

Robotnik said he didn’t want to talk about it, so Stone respected that. Was that a mistake? Should he have pried and asked if the Doctor was sure?

He didn’t even entertain such a thought at the time, you don’t question the Dr. Ivo Robotnik if he’s sure.

And besides that, Stone simply didn’t think at the time that the man reduced to a mewling mess under him the previous night, especially after being the one who initiated, had any regrets about it, sue him.

Can you sue your previous self? This was America, if it was possible anywhere it would be here.

He was suddenly roused from his thoughts, “Stone it’s after five.”

What was Robotnik insinuating?

“It is, sir.”

Robotnik was still turned to his monitors, “You can go home, you know?”

Stone’s heart shattered just a bit more, but he kept his composure, “I don’t leave until you no longer need me, sir.”

Which usually meant until Robotnik passed out at 4am, at which point Stone would either drape a blanket over him or carry him to bed, depending on how deep his sleep appeared to be.

Robotnik’s voice was carefully flat, “Then I’m telling you to go home for the day.”

Stone hesitated, last time he didn’t question Robotnik they ended up here, but…

He settled on a more diplomatic option, “Is there any particular reason for this?”

Robotnik bristled in his seat, but still didn’t turn to him. He was quiet for a few moments, which was a bad sign, Ivo Robotnik never needed to think through his words.

“Because I said so.”

The way it came out and the way the Doctor fidgeted in his chair immediately after saying it only told Stone that that wasn’t exactly how he wanted that sentence to happen, but it was the best he could manage.

Stone was making a mistake right now. He was sure of it. But an order was an order.

“If that is what you want sir.”

On the way from the lab to his small, government-issued apartment there was only really one prevalent thought on Stone’s mind, with several offshoots stemming from it.

Something was terribly wrong between him and the Doctor.

Not a new thought, to be sure, but the thing was, agent Stone knew Dr. Ivo Robotnik. Dr. Ivo Robotnik didn’t mince his words.

The way Stone was just sent home? Borderline linguistic tartare.

Which was… concerning. To say the least.

It was wildly out of character. Hell, what hasn’t been out of character these two weeks? Stone thought that finally his wildest of dreams came true, that the great Ivo Robotnik, God walking amongst men, blessed him with a modicum of affection, the tiniest of acknowledgement of his existence amongst the other specks in the universe.

Even the vague memories he held of their night were enough to satiate his one-sided affection for the rest of time. But of course, that would require their dynamic returning to normal.

How could Stone have been so blind! Two weeks without a single grab by his neck? Something was obviously wrong. (He didn’t realise how much he missed the manhandling until now… He really needed to get a grip.) He was so high on the mere possibility of an evolution in their relationship he somehow stumbled across the path to losing it all.

He couldn’t lose the Doctor like this. The mere thought made mortifying dread pool in his stomach as he stared blankly into the impersonal white ceiling of his bedroom.

If it was anything else that made the Doctor cast Stone away, he would be willing to do it in a heartbeat. Target practice for the badniks? Of course. No longer being deemed useful? The mere insinuation of him being useful at one point would have been a privilege. Fuck, if Robotnik put a gun to his head, Stone would offer to do it himself, the Doctor needn’t bother with something so menial, after all.

But this? What was he supposed to think? Was it a test? Oh, how Stone hoped it was a test. ‘Response to complete lack of contact after an intimate experience’ or something brilliant and unexpected like that.

But the way the Doctor paused as he chose his words?

…

Stone didn’t get much sleep that night.

Chapter 3 – Robotnik – too many mornings after

 

Three weeks. That’s how long Robotnik has let Stone’s little charade continue.

And what a charade it was, complete with sad, wet eyes, comparable only to a kitten found in a dumpster during a flood, whenever Robotnik didn’t berate him against a wall where appropriate or even over something like not taking the coffee from his hand himself.

How pathetic. And to think he has let himself be deceived for what? Almost a decade at this point? (How proud had he been to know their time together to the tenth of a millisecond, now the thought makes this… weight fall over him.)

Some of it had to be real. After all, 9 years 8 months 3 days and 12 hours (approximately) of straight acting was unrealistic, even for someone so clearly dedicated to it. This wasn’t the Truman Show for crying out loud! (Stone kept insisting he looked like the main actor, personally Robotnik didn’t see it.) (No! Stop thinking about him being fun to be around! He’s a traitor!)

Maybe Robotnik was just the tiny bit overreacting… But what other option was there? Clearly there was an outside motive for Stone staying so long.

Robotnik would have noticed material stealing, so no on that. Maybe intelligence gathering? Yes, that could be it. That had to be it. It was why he had him leave at his actual on-paper end-of-shift for the past week. (Ugh, the wet lost dog eyes that always brought.) But who for? He would have noticed the G.U.N. warmongers complain if he had anything but a perfect record, which a data leak to enemy forces would, presumably, tarnish.

Could he be working on his own? To what end? Yes, if his records weren’t falsified, Stone had quite the intellect about him (Nowhere near Robotnik’s, of course, but still.), but intelligence alone didn’t mean anything, it just gave someone the means for greatness. However, by all outside views Stone had seemed perfectly content to stay Robotnik’s sycophant until the end of time and perhaps even a bit after that to make sure everything was taken care of properly.

As much as Robotnik avoided his, eugh, coworkers (on paper at least), he wasn’t deaf when he did encounter them. He knew what crude jokes they made when they thought he wasn’t listening.

It would seem the jokes were proven at least partially true now, wouldn’t it. Not exactly ‘boning on the daily’ as that one sergeant so eloquently put it, but ah well to give credit where it’s due it did happen once.

Not like he would go and correct the sergeant, not that he (or anyone) really could, as the man had that unfortunate accident (rest in pieces) later the very same month he made the comment, which could in no way be traced back to Robotnik.

Stone knew better than to leave evidence.

That was another candidate down, wasn’t it. The amount of times Stone had made someone in ‘their’ organisation disappear at Robotnik’s behest was far too many from far too high places. G.U.N. would never allow that.

Eliminating G.U.N. from his suspect list unfortunately also eliminated the theory that so far appealed most to Robotnik. (Well appealed to his unfortunate romantic inclinations towards Stone, or who he thought was Stone, at least.) That the agent was simply to infiltrate Robotnik’s good graces, and from there able to shoot him in the back at the government’s convenience.

“Your 17:00 latte, sir.”

Robotnik had to once again resist the muscle memory of reaching blindly for the exact spot the latte was always held at. He was like a cursed Pavlov’s dog.

Always a perfect 121 degrees horizontally, then a slight tilt of the wrist either left or right depending on if the coffee was in a mug or a to-go-cup (which itself was based on if the mobile lab was stationary or on the move), height and angle perfectly adjusted by Stone to where Robotnik was currently sitting.

It was a show of weakness he couldn’t afford right now.

He gestured for Stone to leave the coffee on the table, only reaching over once he heard the soft thump of the to-go cup against the desk.

A habit he couldn’t resist however was taking off the lid to look at the always-present latte art.

A depiction of him hugging a badnik with hearts both around them and in the badnik’s optic. It was adorable. It was perfect. Like the rest of his barna- no. He was a traitor, Robotnik couldn’t slip with any affectionate nicknames even within the safety of his genius mind.

Robotnik held the cup tightly as something finally snapped in him.

The sentence came out with an unplanned air of vulnerability and tiredness, “You can stop pretending, you know?”

He didn’t dare look up, instead tracing the small, meticulously painted hearts in the foam with his eyes. He heard the agent bristle, unusual for him, he tends to keep his fidgets consistently quiet like with the rest of his movement.

His tone was flat, carefully so, shocked to be caught in his act after all these years, clearly.

“Pretending about what sir?”

Robotnik couldn’t hold back the groan that escaped him, he turned around in his chair and got up, nose to nose with Stone for the first time in what felt like forever. It made a wave of comfort settle over him briefly, his eyes subconsciously raking over his agent’s body hungrily, before he snapped out of it with renewed anger.

This type of distraction is exactly why he couldn’t allow himself this… weakness! Ever since their eloping it was as if Robotnik was just one of the animalistic sex-driven masses, his eyes never used to lustfully wander like this.

“This!” he gesticulated with the cup, “And that hgnn- stupid smile of yours! Would you- Can you- “

Robotnik scoffed at his inability to form words, he mentally slapped himself, why was he mincing words like some common, feelings-obsessed meathead? He was Dr. Ivo Robotnik! He didn’t beat around the bush and he certainly didn’t aim to spare people’s feelings no matter how flustered they made him.

“Just stop acting like you care!” is what he lamely ground out, finally locking eyes with Stone.

Those stupid, emotional, big, beautiful eyes, while not tearful per-se as of right now, were distinctly getting that kind of emotional shine to them.

There was a slight tremor in the agent’s voice, his expression a taken aback confusion, “I don’t… what?”

“Don’t play dumb, it’s beneath you. Or was that also part of the ruse, hm? I’ll give you credit, it can’t be easy to act more respectful than you really are.”

His brows were a bit furrowed in a clear show of upset, “I’m not pretending.”

“Gah- ‘I’m not pretending’ he says. Obviously you are, nobody could ever withstand my presence for more than strictly necessary. Why should you be the exception? It took what happened between us for me to finally understand: you’re playing some kind of long game, the details of which I will learn, you can be sure of that, agent.”

Stone’s face was progressively crumpling into just the most pathetic look throughout Robotnik’s explanation, but he would not be swayed. He had to let the logical part of his brain reign supreme; it was already bad enough that that look even gave him pause.

“After so long plotting in my shadow I would’ve thought you knew that I don’t take kindly to betrayal, so you might as well make it easier for yourself and just spill now. Let me guess, gaining my trust, inserting yourself into every facet of my daily life and then after learning all the activation codes and loopholes in my programs backstab me and take over? Minus points for unoriginality, but you are rather persis-”

“It’s not an act!” Stone basically cried, his posture tense, his eyes finally shiny with tears.

Impossible. It had to have been an act. That was the only logical conclusion. Strange Stone would be so adamant about it now that the jig was up though. Maybe it was sunk cost fallacy, after all he had been in Robotnik’s employ for 9 years, 8 months 3 days, 12 hours 13 minutes and approximately 8 seconds (and counting), that was a sizeable portion of both their lives.

They were still standing close, not as close as when Robotnik began his impromptu speech but closer than they were in weeks. Despite trying to keep the emotional part (and even worse the part that desired Stone carnally) of his brain under lock and key their proximity was starting to become distracting.

Robotnik huffed with faux annoyance, readying another round of cutting words, the prep of which was also interrupted.

“Please sir, I-I don’t- ” He was clearly desperate. Not even using full sentences? Please.

But credit where credit’s due, Stone was better at emotional manipulation than Robotnik previously estimated, as the move did make Robotnik’s stomach flip in uncomfortable ways, even while being aware of it being an act.

“What made you think this sir? I-” he seemed to steel himself, inhaling shakily, “What is your evidence?”

“Hm?”

“You said our… encounter made you realise my affection for you was a front. What exactly about it?”

Heh, clearly plotting to improve his tactics for future use, although… he seemed so genuinely confused… of course Robotnik knew he couldn’t trust Stone’s expressions but…

Couldn’t hurt to indulge him, could it?

Robotnik answered, his confident front wavering somewhat, “You were so clearly… unaffected.”

(“Unaffected?” “Want an explanation? Don’t interrupt.” “Sorry sir.”)

“You were just acting like it was any other day, which was welcome at first, obviously you know better than to start talking about feelings, but you became sloppy on the final delivery, agent. I mean come ON! Giving such an obvious out straight off the cuff? And with a lone badnik as the latte art! I mean please.”

It really was obvious when he laid it all out like that, and clearly Stone thought so too, his face finally lighting up in some kind of understanding. It made Robotnik’s chest feel heavy, which in turn made him mentally chastise himself, he can’t break now that he’s so close to getting Stone to admit his trickery and subsequently out of his sight.

The agent better not make this difficult, even though Robotnik’s intellect is unparalleled, he was unfortunately still a human, an issue that seems to have become an ever-difficult hurdle lately. And Stone’s acting really was top-notch. Made it hard to concentrate.
